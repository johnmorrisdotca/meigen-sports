# Coverage of people: births and deaths, by sport and by day

Generated by `python3 -I pipeline/coverage_people.py`. Do not edit the numbers by hand.

The package holds **57,798 births and deaths** with a day-precision date, of **187 sports**: 12,876 from SPX's own archive and the priority list, and **44,922 from the Wikidata dump** of 2026-10-12 (`wikidata-20261012-all.json.gz`, CC0, each with its Q id). A person is ranked by the number of Wikipedia editions he has; below 30 editions a day, sport and kind keeps its best two with at least 10 editions (five fewer with an Olympic medal or a Hall of Fame behind him). Wikidata dates are used only at day precision on the Gregorian calendar, from 1850, and never on January 1, which Wikidata writes for a year-only date about as often as for a real one.

## By day

- Births and deaths per day: min 54, median 158, max 193.
- Sports per day: min 6, median 52, max 63.
- Every one of the 366 days has people; the fewest are 01-01 (54), 02-29 (65), 03-22 (130), 04-21 (131), 07-06 (131).
- January 1 holds only the births and deaths that were confirmed before (the English Wikipedia lead states the day): 54 people in 6 sports. It is the one day with a gap.

## The gaps

- **January 1**, as said above.
- A sport below ten people has no more than the Wikipedia editions allow; a person with fewer than ten editions (five with an Olympic medal or a Hall of Fame) is not published, because a short article is not fame.
- A person whose first occupation is not a sport one, or whose description opens with another trade (a president, an actor), is not published as a sports person; a Julian date is not converted, so it is left out.
- Para and wheelchair sports, and a description that names a crime, are not published (John's rule: no para label, no health).
