# Changelog

Changes to the package are recorded here. Dates use ISO 8601.

## [Unreleased]

- The demo's header takes `@johnmorrisdotca/kazoku-ui` ^0.12 (a dev dependency, used by the demo only): the language picker and theme switch, with the zoom slider and Centre button of 0.11 and 0.12 not used here (no turnable view). Nothing in the package changes.

## [0.27.0] - 2026-11-03

- Feature (MODULES-250): one fame list. `data-sources/priority-people.csv` (was `people.csv`) ranks all 2,000 people 1 to 2000 by world fame: the score is the person's Wikipedia language editions, plus 2 for each Olympic medal (5 count), 4 for each world title (3 count) and 8 for each hall of fame (2 count), so the editions lead and the rest adds at most 38 (`docs/fame.md`, the weights in `data-sources/fame-rule.json`, `pipeline/people/fame.py`). The old 999 order is only a tie-break, never a floor: 232 of the first 999 are at or above their old place, 767 below it. 39 people cannot be scored (30 have no Wikidata item yet, 9 are listed under another person's item) and keep their place from the first list. No sport or country holds more than 35 or 45 places of any block of 100 ranks; the score alone keeps inside, so nobody was moved. The first twenty: Lionel Messi, Cristiano Ronaldo, Muhammad Ali, Michael Schumacher, Pelé, Roger Federer, Diego Maradona, Jackie Chan, Novak Djokovic, Rafael Nadal, Michael Jordan, Edmund Hillary, Usain Bolt, Zinedine Zidane, Serena Williams, Mike Tyson, Kylian Mbappé, Ronaldo Nazário, Franz Beckenbauer, The Rock.
- Ranking: a birth or a death of a listed person carries `fameRank` (new field) and `priorityAthlete` is now true for all 2,000 (it was the first 999). `significance` adds 50 for ranks 1-100, 45 to 300, 38 to 700, 28 to 1,200 and 18 to 2,000, where it added a flat 45 (20 for a thin fill); a fact with `priorityAthlete` and no `fameRank` keeps 45. A list without `keepLegends` (the day's people shard) keeps its one-person rule: a legend may be listed twice only where legends are held by no cap. The offline set is 2,369 facts (was 2,337): 250 facts left it and 282 joined, nearly all births and deaths ordered by world fame instead of the first list (17 finals left, 8 joined). The golden facts all hold.
- Catalogue: `catalogue.json` has a `fame` section (the rule, the spread of the first 20, 100, 500 and all, and the 2,000 people as `[rank, qid, name, sport, country, score]`); the catalogue page shows the first 100.
- Retired (MODULES-243): `extract_wikidata_famous.json` and `pipeline/fetch_wikidata_open.py` (a fetch of about 500 famous people, a subset of the list; no fact came from it), `data-sources/priority-athletes.csv` and `pipeline/priority_athletes.py` (the 999 are the rows of `priority` 1 in the new file). The rebuild no longer reads the famous extract (`pnpm data:facts` lists 22 extracts and 10 repository files); `meta.topFamous` and the showcase feed's birthdays come from the first 80 people of the fame list. `test/fame.test.ts` proves nothing reads the old files.
- Data: the facts are rebuilt from the same extracts as 0.26.0 (126,810 facts, none lost, no id changed); only `fameRank` and `priorityAthlete` are new on 2,288 births and deaths. The guard's allowances (`data-sources/rebuild-allow.json`) list exactly those two fields.
- Tests: `test/fame.test.ts` (each person once, ranks 1 to 2000 once each, the score reproduced from the columns and the rule file, the tie-break, the unscored, the spread rule, the bonus in `significance`, the catalogue's list), `pipeline/people/test_fame.py`, the catalogue page's fame panel in `e2e/catalogue.demo.ts`.
- Docs only (MODULES-247): two more guides, with Japanese twins: architecture (code, the package's data and the published feed kept apart; the path from a date to a sentence; the rules and the checks) and from a date to a sentence; `demo/builder.html` and `demo/builder.ts`, a page where the day, the calendar page, the layout, the language and the number of facts are chosen and the code that makes the card is copied, with `e2e/builder.demo.ts`; eleven more README examples. The pictures' stamp (`docs/images/stamp.json`) is written again because `demo/` and `scripts/site.ts` are among its inputs; no picture is drawn differently.

- Rebuild: `pnpm data:facts` refuses to run, naming each file, when an extract is missing, empty, older than an extract it follows or made for other people than the extracts now name; `data-sources/facts/inputs.json` records the SHA-256 of every input and the day of the rebuild. The one extract allowed to be absent is `extract_wikidata_famous.json` (no published fact comes from it), and `extract_drafts.json` may be empty. A rebuild guard (`pipeline/rebuild_guard.py`) compares the new facts with those of the last release tag and stops, writing nothing, on a lost fact, a changed id, a category with fewer facts, a field that changed on a fact that kept its id or a fact of the offline set that is lost, unless `data-sources/rebuild-allow.json` lists it with a reason. `pnpm test:rebuild` and `test/rebuild-guard.test.ts` hold the rules; `pipeline/README.md` has the order of a rebuild.
- When a person is held by DS1, its row replaces the dump's or the priority list's, and now takes from them what it lacks: the epithet of a person without words, the birth place, the death date, the birth and age of a death, and the number of Wikipedia editions. A person in a para sport is published only when the para pass chose him; a person of an unlisted para or Deaflympic sport is not. A position code that does not belong to the person's sport (a basketball code on a football player) is left out; `LN` and `LS` are in the table of football positions.
- Ranking: a game of the AHL, ECHL, NCAAF, PWHL or NWSL counts for less (`significance`), so it does not outrank the opening of a famous venue.
- Not carried over: 39 NBA playoff games that DS1 no longer lists as finals, 30 NBA club identities dated 27 April 2012 that DS1 dropped (every club on the same day), 13 MLB, NBA, AFL and NFL games (eight doubleheader or suspended games whose Retrosheet row DS1 merged into an SPX row, not a publishable source here; five that DS1 no longer holds or no longer features), the two para or Deaflympic people above. Each is listed with its reason in `data-sources/rebuild-allow.json` and reported to the database's owner.
- Feature: `catalogue.json` (`@johnmorrisdotca/meigen-sports/catalogue.json`, and `data/v1/0.1.0/catalogue.json` on Pages) counts everything the data holds, from the facts themselves, in the build: totals, facts by kind, sport, league, decade, year and calendar day (the least, the most, the empty and the thin days), the languages, the people (deceased, and "not known to be deceased", never "living"), the events, the sources with their licences, the extracts with their checksums, the bundled offline set, the gaps (what is thin, partial or waiting) and the rules in force. The type is `Catalogue`. A test counts it again from the facts.
- Demo: a "What is inside" panel on the page (totals and the gaps one tap away) and a page of charts, `catalogue.html`, drawn once from `catalogue.json` as static SVG, no library: facts by kind, sport, decade and year, a heat map of the 366 days (thin days outlined), people by sport, the sources, the offline set against the full set, and the gaps; each chart has its numbers in a table behind it; light and dark, English and Japanese. `e2e/catalogue.demo.ts` checks that every chart is drawn.
- Tests: the Allegheny Club, the corrected games, the football positions, the person-to-DS1 hand-over, the catalogue (`test/catalogue.test.ts`), the guard (`pipeline/test_rebuild_guard.py`); the golden list's check day is 2026-11-03 and 11 paths that facts lost to ranking (a venue's opening, two WNBA and CFL births, a team page) are not locked again.
