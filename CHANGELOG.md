# Changelog

Changes to the package are recorded here. Dates use ISO 8601.

## [Unreleased]

## [0.9.0] - 2026-10-21

- Wording: a fact worded for its own day states its year. "Mickey Mantle ... was born 95 years ago today." is now "... was born 95 years ago today, in 1931." (English and Japanese: `95年前の今日、1931年に生まれた`); "Today is the birthday of X" says "born in 1931"; "X turns 65 today" says "(born in 1961)"; an event whose name holds no year says "In 1995, Channel One Russia began in Moscow."; the Games named 2020 and held in 2021 say "In 2021, the 2020 Summer Olympics began." `test/year-visible.test.ts` renders every template that can say each of 20,000+ facts on its own day and every bundled history row, in both languages, and fails if one of them leaves the year out.
- Wording: a person with a club and no position is "of the club", not "the club" (`Gregg Popovich, of the San Antonio Spurs, was born ...`; 1,859 facts said "the San Antonio Spurs," as if a club were what he is). The best-known club is the one he spent most seasons at: a stint written as one span with another club's years inside it is shared (Mark Messier is the Edmonton Oilers' 12 seasons, not the Rangers' 1991 to 2003 with Vancouver in the middle); 173 of 5,936 people with several clubs changed, among the 43 most famous only Messier and Manny Ramirez (8 seasons at each club). Wikidata's "association football player" is "footballer" everywhere (3,500 facts, `Bosnian footballer`): the description path of the 0.7.0 rule accepted the person's own words as they were. Baseball's playoffs are `ポストシーズン` in Japanese (hockey and basketball keep `プレーオフ`). `test/golden/famous-clubs.json` holds 38 famous people, by Wikidata id, to the club each is best known for; the facts John saw on the demo (`Wayne Gretzky, the Winnipeg Jets`, `Winner of the Vezina Trophy`) are not in 0.8.1 or the Pages data: they came from a build of 2026-10-16, and `test/wording-regression.test.ts` holds that they cannot come back.
- Japanese, after a review (docs/translations.md, "Japanese revised in 0.9.0"; not yet read by a person who reads Japanese): the colon of a "years ago today" sentence is a comma, the place of an event follows its name (`1995年、Channel One Russia（Moscow）が始まった`), a Games named for another year is `2020年大会`.
- Tags: every fact carries, in `tags`, the namespaced tags its own fields give (`src/tags.ts`, `derivedTags`; the history types are as they were): `sport:`, `league:`, `competition:`, `series:` (olympics, world-cup, world-championships), `country:`, `host:`, `birthcountry:`, `team:`, `person:`, `position:`, `venue:`, `decade:`, `century:`, `phase:`, `stage:`, `award:`, `title:`, `honour:` (award-winner, champion, hall-of-fame) and `source:`; counts per namespace are in [docs/tags.md](docs/tags.md) (generated). Nothing is guessed, and no tag says sex, gender, ethnicity, religion, politics, family or health (`test/tags.test.ts` fails on a namespace or value that does). Ranking counts the kind of fact only (`kindTagsOf`), so the day shards, the feeds and the offline set hold the same facts as in 0.8.1 (all 1,098 day, people and events shards are unchanged in their ids). `tagsOf` lists every tag; `FactFilters` gained `tags`, `host` and `birthCountry`, and `country` now finds a person's country too. The tag grammar of a loaded fact (`isFactRecord`) accepts `namespace:value`.
- Countries: 74,147 facts have a country: a person's `countries` (every country he represents in sport or holds, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, or ISO 3166-3 for a former country Hata has a flag for: 2,024 facts of the Soviet Union, 526 of East Germany, 514 of Czechoslovakia ...), a game's two teams. 54,251 have a `birthCountry` and 22,831 events a `hostCountries`. `flag` (and `flagOf`) is a flag code only when it is unambiguous (the one country he represents in sport, or his only one; two teams of one country): 69,031 facts, none for two countries with none represented, a state no code names, or a game between two countries; 5,907 people have two or more countries. 210 country codes; the top fifteen are US 31,945, CA 4,602, DE 2,919, GB 2,903, FR 2,721, IT 2,403, JP 2,141, RU 2,058, SUHH 2,024, SE 1,531, NL 1,388, ES 1,309, AU 1,261, NO 1,115 and HU 978. England, Scotland and Wales are `GB` with `GB-ENG`, `GB-SCT`, `GB-WLS` as the flag. New extractor `pipeline/wikidata_links.py` (the existing extracts are untouched; it adds `extract_wikidata_links.json`, 6 tests in `pipeline/test_wikidata_links.py`).
- Links: 86,003 facts name the Wikidata item they are about (`wikidata`: the person, or the event); 85,145 carry the title of its English Wikipedia article, 68,459 the French and 34,278 the Japanese (`wikipedia`: titles, never addresses). `wikipediaUrl(fact, language)` builds the address in the reader's language, English next, then Wikidata, and `null` for a fact with no item (a game, a venue, a person with no Q id: no link is guessed from a name).
- Queries and indexes (static files only): `indexes/countries`, `birthcountries`, `hosts`, `decades`, `awards`, `positions`, `honours`, `series` and `titles` (ids), `feeds/countries/XX.json` (a country's best forty), `index/vocabulary.json` (every value with its count, for menus), `index/teams.json`, and `index/names/<letter>.json` (every person by the first letter of his first and of his last name). `queryFacts({ country: "RO", day: "03-04" }, load)` intersects the indexes and loads the store files the ids are in (`storeBucket`: plain SHA-256, tested against Node's); `loadCountry`, `loadCountryIds`, `queryFactIds`, `loadFactsById`, `findPeopleByName`, `loadVocabulary`, `loadTeamPicker`. The facts on Pages are 200 MB (were 148), the offline set 2.1 MB (was 1.6), the package about 1.1 MB packed.
- Demo: the year of the card's fact is on the leaf under the day, the tags are listed under every fact (one scrolling line on the card), a small `Wikipedia` link (ja: `ウィキペディア`, `ウィキペディア（英語版）` where only the English article exists) opens the article of the person or event in a new tab (`rel="noopener noreferrer"`); "My favourites" is a set of filters kept in the address (`?country=RO&decade=1990s&person=Q209518&day=1`): sport, league, history tag, country, decade, award, honour, years, a club and a person picked by name (searchable lists, by keyboard or tap), and "only the day above". The form controls use the page's colour tokens, so they are readable on the light theme (they were near-black), in the dark one, and with each of the five cloths: `e2e/explore.demo.ts` measures the contrast of every control (at least 4.5) in Chromium and WebKit. The card's words are checked not to clip at 900 to 1920 px, and the filter grid no longer overflows a phone.
- Data: the stored sentences are rebuilt from the revised templates (no fact added or removed: 103,592). `data:facts` rebuilds from the existing extracts and `extract_wikidata_links.json`.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-10-21

- Data: the events and competition editions of every sport, from the same Wikidata dump (CC0). 28,205 facts in 185 sports, each with its Q id (`wikidata:competition-end:Q176883:2010-07-11`): 11,729 that began, 11,614 that ended and 4,862 that took place on one day, 5,951 with a winner and 19,204 with a place, as Wikidata states them. The Olympic Games (Athens 1896 to Paris 2024), the World Cups, the Grand Prix, the Giro, Wimbledon, the Chess Olympiad, Super Bowl I. 103,592 facts now (was 75,387): a day has 76 events (median; 9 to 153) of 27 sports (median; 4 to 50). The offline set is unchanged in size (1,510 facts, 1.6 MB), the package is about 1 MB packed, and the facts on Pages are 148 MB (were 113 MB).
- Three new categories (`EXTRACT_CATEGORIES`), tagged `competition-boundary` (and `championship-win` with a winner at a final or a championship, `combat-sport-result` for a bout or a fight card, `exceptional-achievement` for a perfect game): `competition-start` (the start of an edition), `competition-end` (its end, with the winner where Wikidata names one) and `sport-event` (a single day). New fields `eventLabel` and `eventPlace`; `winnerName` is the winner of an event as well as of an award. The sentences reuse the types `competition-start`, `competition-end` and `event-date` with nine new patterns in English and in Japanese: "On May 29, 2012, the 2012 Rhythmic Gymnastics European Championships began in Nizhny Novgorod.", "On July 30, 1966, the 1966 FIFA World Cup Final took place at Wembley Stadium, won by England." ("the" before a name that opens with a year or a number; "at" before a venue and "in" before a town; a name that opens with a small letter is capitalized where it opens a sentence; a name that ends in a full stop does not double the sentence's).
- New feed: `facts/events/MM-DD.json`, a day's events across sports (up to 40, at most three of one sport), loaded by `loadFactEventsDay`; the demo shows it under the day's people.
- What is left out, and why (`pipeline/wikidata_dump_events.py`, each rule counted in its report): a date on January 1 or December 31 (Wikidata writes a season or a year-only date that way), a season or a play-off and the editions of the leagues SPX's own archive holds (NHL, NBA, NFL, MLB, CFL, MLS), para, wheelchair, deaf and blind sport, an edition whose end is before its start (50 items), a name that says "today" or "tonight", an item with fewer than five Wikipedia editions, and one with fewer than twenty unless it is among the best two of its day, sport and kind. An event keeps the name it is called by (the 2022 FIFA Women's World Cup is that), but no person is told to be of a sex: where the name carries a sex marker a winner who is a person is not named, and a national team is named by its country ("England men's national association football team" is "England").
- Ranking: an event starts at 30 and gains by the editions of its page (5 for ten, 10 for twenty, 16 for thirty, 28 for fifty, 45 for a hundred), and 6 for a winner, so a Games opening ranks with a person known in fifty languages and a routine race below the opening of a stadium. The golden floor is as it was.
- Golden suite: 316 facts the data must hold (was 279): 37 events added (the openings of 15 Olympic Games, 11 World Cups, Super Bowl I and the 1966 final, a Grand Prix, the first Chess Olympiad, a Giro, Wimbledon, the French Open, the European Games, a judo World Championships), each date the dump's own.
- Tests: `test/dump-events.test.ts` holds the events (Q id, CC0 source, a real day, none on a year's edge, an end after its start, a name, a place and a winner only where the item has them, no person told to be of a sex, the events shard of each day, the provenance in the manifest, the sentences in both languages); `pipeline/test_wikidata_dump_events.py` holds the extractor on a hand-written dump (8 tests); `docs/coverage-events.md` is generated from the facts (`pnpm data:coverage-events`).


## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-21

- Data: the births and deaths of sports people of every sport, from the one-off Wikidata dump of 2026-10-12 (CC0). 44,922 facts (32,972 births, 11,950 deaths) in 146 sports, each with its Q id (`wikidata:player-birthday:Q42:1952-03-11`), a sport, an ISO nationality code, a birthplace where the item has one and a short phrase, the new `epithet` ("Brazilian footballer", "Olympic wrestler"). 75,387 facts now (was 30,480) and 57,798 births and deaths in 187 sports, on all 366 days: a day has 158 (median; 54 to 193), of 52 sports (median; 6 to 63). The offline set is still three to five facts for each day, 1,510 facts and 1.6 MB (was 1,509 and 1.6 MB); the package is about 1 MB packed; the facts on Pages are 113 MB (were 54 MB).
- Provenance: the dump's name, date, md5 and sha256 and the sha256 of each filtered file read are in `facts/manifest.json` under `sources.wikidataDump`, with the number of facts it added; `NOTICE.md` and `data-sources/sources.json` name the source.
- Who is published, and who is not (`pipeline/wikidata_dump_people.py`, each rule counted in its report): a sports person is one whose first occupation is a sport occupation and whose English description does not open with another trade (a president, an actor), or whose description opens with a sport role. A date is used only at day precision on the Gregorian calendar, from 1850 and never January 1: Wikidata writes a year-only date as January 1 about as often as a real one (about 3,700 of the 6,765 births it holds on that day, by the excess over an ordinary day), and the dump holds no way to tell them apart, so January 1 holds only what was confirmed before. A Julian date is not converted. A death is `deceased` only when Wikidata holds one; with none the status is `unknown`, never `living`. Fame is the number of Wikipedia editions (`sitelinks`): everyone with 30 or more, and the best two of each day, sport and kind with 10 or more (5 with an Olympic medal or a Hall of Fame). Nothing about sex or gender, ethnicity, religion, politics, family or health is read or stored; a sport label that carries a sex marker is reduced to its sport; para, wheelchair, deaf and blind sports are left out, and so is a person whose description names a crime.
- Words from data: `epithet` is the person's own English description when every word of it is an ordinary word of other sports people's descriptions, else a phrase built from his occupation and country the way those descriptions write them (2,868 of the 47,084 rows the extract makes, 6%). A description is never shown as it is; a misspelling ("Brazilian associaton fooball player", 41 priority people) is replaced by the built phrase. `epithet` is said where a record has no club, position or `highlight` and adds nothing to a fact's significance.
- New feed: `facts/people/MM-DD.json`, a day's births and deaths across sports (up to 40, at most three of one sport), loaded by `loadFactPeopleDay`. The day's own list (`days/`) is mixed and holds two births and two deaths at most. The demo shows the day's people under the find results.
- Ranking: `significance` grades the editions beyond twenty: 20 more for fifty and 25 more for a hundred, so a name known in a hundred languages that the priority list does not hold ranks as one it does. The priority people with fifty editions or more move up among themselves; Sue Bird (37 editions) gave her October 16 day-list and offline slot to Falcão (52), and the golden floor was re-locked for it. Fifteen facts of nine people the priority list named are gone: they were Wikidata items of a namesake or of a politician, an actor or a doctor (Sun Yat-sen, Ronald Ross, the actress Jennifer Jones, the comedian George Burns and five more).
- Golden suite: 279 facts the data must hold (was 196): 14 known gaps the dump filled (Roger Federer, Arthur Ashe, Eric Liddell, Johnny Weissmuller, Mario Andretti, Danica Patrick, Red Grange, Johnny Unitas, Joe Namath, John Elway, Ronda Rousey and others) moved to the must list, and 69 famous athletes of 40 more sports added by `pipeline/golden/add_from_dump.py` (offline; each date is the dump's own, said in the entry's `verifiedBy`; an entry added there has no `women` flag, since the dump holds no sex or gender). 37 known gaps remain.
- Tests: `test/dump-people.test.ts` holds the new facts (Q id, CC0 source, a real day, no January 1, an age that is possible, `unknown` never `living`, an epithet of ordinary words with no word of sex, religion, politics, family, health or crime, no forbidden field, people of many sports on every day, the people shard, the provenance in the manifest); `pipeline/test_wikidata_dump_people.py` holds the extractor on a hand-written dump (12 tests); `docs/coverage-people.md` is generated from the facts (`pnpm data:coverage-people`).


## [0.6.7] - 2026-10-21

- Fix: a fact shown outside its own day now says its full date. The demo's ten mixed facts showed "Pittsburgh Pirates won 16–4 against New York Giants (MLB, 1945)" and "Lynn Greer ... was born in 1979" with the known day dropped, and "Today is the birthday of Ralph Sampson" in a list on a day that is not his birthday. A sentence now takes its context from `asOf`, the day being shown: on the fact's own day (same month and day) it may say "today", "on this day", "N years ago today" and "turns N" and keeps the year only; on any other day, and when `asOf` is absent, it says the date at the precision the record holds, in English and Japanese ("On July 4, 1945, Pittsburgh Pirates beat New York Giants 16–4 (MLB)."; "Ralph Sampson, the Houston Rockets center, was born on July 7, 1960."; `1945年7月4日、…`; "in July 1945", "in 1945", "around 1945" for a month, a year, an approximate date). A new `day: true` mark on a template says which ones may use "today"; every other template carries one of `{dateLead}`, `{leadIn}`, `{dateOn}`, `{date}`. A January 1 on a franchise, award or draft stays "the year", never "January 1". New exports `dayContext(record, asOf)` and `factDate(record, locale)`; `renderHistory` dates a bundled history row from its own `date`.
- Fix: no placeholder for a missing value is ever printed. A club the archive names "Allegheny Club (none)" is "Allegheny Club" (30 published facts carried it: 29 games and a club identity). `cleanLabel` strips "(none)", "(null)", "(undefined)" and "(n/a)" from every name and field a sentence uses, a name that is only a placeholder yields no sentence, and a finished sentence that still holds "(none)", "null", "undefined" or "NaN" is not returned. The converter strips it from team and club names, so the published `home.name`, `away.name` and `clubName` are clean too.
- Data: every stored sentence (`text`, `textJa`) is the "anywhere" form with the full date, as the 30,480 facts are read on days that are not their own (a team's feed, a favourites list, a random file); the data and the offline set are rebuilt from the same extracts, so only the sentences and the names of those 30 facts changed.
- Tests: `test/dated-facts.test.ts` runs the whole bundled set (30,480 facts in English and Japanese, fixed, seeded and in runs, the offline set, every day shard and feed, 1,052 history rows) and checks that outside its day every sentence holds its date, none says "today" or "on this day", and none holds a placeholder; that on a fact's own day it may say "today"; the precisions (day, month, year, approximate, January 1); and the templates (every one that says "today" is marked, every other holds a date field). The golden suite has two new never-rules, `dated-sentences` and a "(none)" check in `clean-sentences`.
- Demo: each list is worded for the day it is about: the calendar card for today's date, the find list for the picked date, and the ten mixed facts, favourites and the showcase for no day, so none of them says "today".
- Docs: [sentence templates](docs/templates.md) say the two contexts, the four date fields and the missing-value rule (Japanese page and translation table updated; not yet checked by a person who reads Japanese).

## [0.6.3] - 2026-10-20

- Wording: positions are spelled out in every sentence, never as codes. "Mickey Mantle, the New York Yankees CF" is "the New York Yankees center fielder", "Juan González, the Texas Rangers DH" is "the Texas Rangers designated hitter", a hockey "LW" is a left wing and a "G" a goaltender. One table, `src/templates/positions.ts`, picked by the record's sport and then its code (C is a catcher in baseball and a center in hockey, basketball and football), in North American spelling ("center", "defenseman"), with the Japanese wording from the same table. A code the sport's table does not hold is left out of the sentence, never printed or guessed. The record keeps the code. 10,155 birthdays and deaths changed; `test/positions-series.test.ts` fails on any bare code in a published sentence and on any code of the data without a row.
- Wording: a final won over several games says the series result, not the last game's score. "Cincinnati Reds won the 1990 World Series, beating Oakland Athletics 2–1" is now "Cincinnati Reds won the 1990 World Series, sweeping the Oakland Athletics 4–0 with a 2–1 win in Game 4", and a long series reads "beating the Boston Red Sox 4 games to 3 with a 3–2 win in Game 7". The tally (`seriesWinnerWins`, `seriesLoserWins`, `seriesGames`) is counted from the final round's own games and kept only when it agrees with the league's format (four wins, five in the best-of-nine World Series of 1903 and 1919–21); a tied game counts as played, not won (1912). The 2008 World Series is no longer worded as a title: the archive holds a duplicate of the suspended Game 5, so its games do not add up. 175 finals changed (111 World Series, 64 NBA Finals); the football finals and the soccer matches keep their single game's score. Three new patterns for English and for Japanese (`final-series-won`, `final-series-to-win`, `final-series-went-to`); a test holds every series sentence to a tally that agrees with the games.
- Context for finals, read from DS1 (`extract_final_context.sql`, 1,918 finals in one page): a final's sentence takes the deciding game's venue ("at Oakland Coliseum, Oakland"), which title it was for the franchise ("It was their 9th title.") and the winner's regular season ("They had finished the regular season 15–1–0.", W–L, W–L–T or W–L–OTL as the league writes it), each only when its field is present (`venueName`, `venueCity`, `winnerTitleNumber`, `winnerRecord`). All 309 published finals have a venue. A title number is given on 197 (NFL 70, NBA 64, CFL 55, AFL 4, AAFC 3, NHA 1) and a record on 176 (NBA 64, CFL 55, NFL 48 of 70, AFL 4, AAFC 3, NHA 2). The title-count rule is strict, and a missing count is better than a wrong one: it needs every season of every league the club belonged to, up to that year, to have a champion on record or a "not awarded" row (`gapSeasons` = 0), and the winner to be that season's champion. Of the 1,918 games read, 1,213 passed the gap test and 910 got a number; 705 failed it, among them every World Series (32 early MLB seasons have no champion on record: `gapSeasons` 32), so no World Series says which title it was. No World Series carries a regular-season record either: DS1 holds no complete regular-season standing that the extractor accepts for them. 22 of the 70 Super Bowl and NFL title games have no record either (no complete whole-season standing), and the 1914 NHA final has no title number because DS1 does not name its winner (the Canadiens) as the NHA champion of that season: that sentence is likely a game of a two-game series and is left for review. The counts can be added once DS1 holds the champions before 1903 and MLB's whole-season standings.
- Tests: `pnpm test:finalcontext` runs the extractor on a scratch Postgres (port 7403), the converter's series tally has unit tests (extra game, missing game, third club, tie, best-of-nine years), and the data test holds every final's venue, title number and record to its sentence. "an 11–0 win", never "a 11–0 win".

## [0.6.1] - 2026-10-20
